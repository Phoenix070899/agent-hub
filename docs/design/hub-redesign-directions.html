Four directions for the Hub App

Each mock uses the same moment: the lanh session with four Workers, sasuke waiting on a push approval, and the Orchestrator’s latest Summary. Same content, so you’re comparing the look and the layout, nothing else.

Glass has nothing to refract

Liquid Glass is meant for controls floating over rich content. Here the content is an opaque terminal on a flat dark fill, so the capsules render as soft grey pills with no depth cue.

Everything is the same shape

Session cards, tabs, the plus button, Needs cards and the usage footer are all glass capsules. Nothing tells you that Needs you outranks a tab, so urgency is carried by a 12% yellow tint.

The chrome fights the terminal

The window is Ayu Dark inside and system grey glass outside. Two material systems meet at every edge, which is why it reads as a wrapper around a terminal rather than one tool.

My recommendation: A as the base, with B’s Needs strip and lamps. A fixes the material problem at the lowest cost (it’s mostly stock SwiftUI with the glass removed), and B’s strip solves the urgency problem better than any card. Pick C if you want the Summary to be the app, or D if you mostly watch Workers run.
A

Quiet Mac

Opaque, native, three columns

Behave like Xcode or Tower. Glass stays only where macOS puts it (the sidebar material); the terminal runs flush to the edges, and Needs you moves into a right-hand inspector so it never pushes the terminal down.

Why it fits

You live in this window all day. Stock macOS chrome disappears; the terminal gets every remaining pixel and stays visually continuous.

In SwiftUI

  • Remove every .glassEffect
  • .inspector for Needs and Summary
  • Standard List outline, toolbar Picker for tabs

Type and color

SF Pro and JetBrains Mono. System accent; state colors only on small marks.

sidetermneedrun

Trade-off

Least character of the four. It will look like a good Mac app, not like your app.

B

Switchboard

Dense, dark, one surface

The Orchestrator is the operator and each Worker is a line with a lamp. Chrome takes the terminal’s own color so the window is one surface. When something needs you, a lit strip spans the terminal with keys you can hit without leaving the keyboard.

Why it fits

Your Workers are concurrent lines, and the job is noticing which one lit up. A table with lamps scans faster than cards, and it holds 15 Workers without scrolling.

In SwiftUI

  • Table or a custom grid for lines
  • Window background set to the terminal theme
  • Strip is a plain HStack with .keyboardShortcut

Type and color

IBM Plex Sans Condensed for density, JetBrains Mono for ids and the terminal.

baseneedrundonefail

Trade-off

Strongest identity, but dark-only in practice, and the custom font needs bundling. Least Mac-like.

C

Ledger

Light, Summary-first

Your Summary already has a shape: Needs you, Done, Running. Make that the left column across every session, instead of a session tree. The terminal turns light and calm, and anything waiting on you is marked with a highlighter, the way you’d mark a printout.

Why it fits

It organises the app around the question you actually ask when you look over: what needs me, what finished. Sessions become a filter, not a tree.

In SwiftUI

  • Sectioned List fed by all snapshots
  • Light terminal theme (Tango Light exists)
  • Highlight via AttributedString background

Type and color

Instrument Sans for UI, Spline Sans Mono for the terminal. Ink blue on cool paper.

deskinkmarkerrun

Trade-off

Light terminals are a hard sell for some agent CLIs’ colors, and it needs a matching dark variant to be complete.

D

Wall

Every live Worker on screen

Closest to Orca’s spirit. The Orchestrator is pinned on the left and live Workers tile on the right, so you watch them work instead of clicking into them. A Worker that needs you gets a lit frame and answers inline, in its own tile.
